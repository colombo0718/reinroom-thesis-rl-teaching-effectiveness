## 中文摘要

強化學習（Reinforcement Learning, RL）已成為人工智慧領域的核心技術之一，惟其概念抽象、入門門檻高，難以下放至程式背景有限的學習者。本研究自行開發 **Rein Room（RR）**——一套純前端、零安裝、瀏覽器直接運行之視覺化互動式 RL 教學平台，並以「Colab + Gymnasium」程式碼導向工具作為對照組，於同一所大學之兩個資訊類班級進行**準實驗（quasi-experimental）**研究，評估 RR 平台於 RL 教學上之初步成效。

實驗以 RR 組與 Colab 組兩班國際學生為對象，於兩天連續課程（Day 1 概念導入、Day 2 進階任務）中執行同一套教學節奏與評量工具。本研究於正式分析中限定有效樣本為「前後測皆完成且 Student ID 可成功配對」之**配對樣本**（RR 組 n = 10、Colab 組 n = 12）。考量小樣本下常態分配假設難以驗證，量表推論統計採用 Wilcoxon signed-rank 檢定與 Mann-Whitney U 檢定並報告效果量 r；任務完成率採用卡方檢定（含 Fisher exact）並報告 Cramer's V。

主要發現如下：
（1）**理論知識測驗**：Colab 組於前後測達統計顯著進步（W = 5.0, p = .012 \*, r = .756 大），RR 組於本配對樣本下未達統計顯著（W = 3.0, p = .113, r = .647 大）；組間增益分數差異未達顯著。
（2）**任務完成率**：T1–T5 五項任務之完成率於兩組間皆未達統計顯著差異；T5 Fighter 自主超參數任務於 RR 組（61.1%）與 Colab 組（25.0%）間之描述差距其效果量 V = .286（中等），於現有樣本規模下未達 α = .05 顯著水準。
（3）**任務完成時間**：T4 Heli 主評量任務 RR 組（17.6 分鐘）顯著短於 Colab 組（53.0 分鐘）（U = 0, p = .0013 \*\*, r = .813 大）；T5 Fighter 任務 RR 組（20.4 分鐘）亦顯著短於 Colab 組（33.3 分鐘）（U = 0, p = .0115 \*, r = .687 大，惟 Colab n = 3 之穩健性限制需注意）。
（4）**主觀量表**：平台回饋量表五題、系統易用性量表、心智負荷量表整體與多數分項，於配對樣本下皆未達統計顯著；其中心智負荷量表「挫折感」分項為唯一達顯著者，方向為 RR 組顯著高於 Colab 組（U = 92.0, p = .034 \*, r = .450 中）。
（5）**質性開放題**：於「最困難的部分」中，Colab 組於「參數調整困難」與「圖表判讀困難」之主題提及比例（42%、42%）為 RR 組（17%、17%）之 2.5 倍。

依上述結果，本研究貢獻可定位為三層次：（1）建置可瀏覽器執行、無需安裝環境之強化學習教學平台 Rein Room；（2）以教學介入標準化措施完成兩種教學介面之小樣本準實驗對照，於行為層次（完成時間）呈現 RR 即時視覺化介面之效率優勢，於主觀量表層次呈現 RR 早期版本資訊密度過高之副作用；（3）整理五項對未來「視覺化互動式教學平台」設計者之具體建議（資訊密度控制、教室模式、遷移能力評量、即時調整介面、同儕互動機制）。本研究樣本規模於量表面向上之檢定力受限，相關結論之推論範圍應局限於本研究情境。

**關鍵字：** 強化學習、互動式教學平台、視覺化學習、AI 教育、準實驗研究、Rein Room

## Abstract

Reinforcement Learning (RL) has become one of the core technologies in artificial intelligence, yet its abstract concepts and high programming threshold make it difficult to deliver to learners with limited coding background. This study develops **Rein Room (RR)** — a pure front-end, zero-installation, browser-based visual interactive platform for RL education — and conducts a **quasi-experimental** study against a conventional code-driven approach (Google Colab + Gymnasium) at a single university with two computer-science classes, evaluating the initial teaching effectiveness of the RR platform.

The experiment involved two classes of international students over two consecutive class days (Day 1 concept introduction, Day 2 advanced tasks), with identical teaching pace and assessment instruments. Formal analyses were restricted to **matched samples** where both pre- and post-tests were completed and Student IDs could be successfully matched (RR group n = 10, Colab group n = 12). Given the difficulty of validating normality assumptions with small samples, this study employed Wilcoxon signed-rank and Mann-Whitney U tests with effect size r; task completion rates were analyzed with chi-square (with Fisher exact when applicable) and Cramer's V.

Key findings are as follows:
(1) **Concept tests**: The Colab group showed statistically significant improvement from pre- to post-test (W = 5.0, p = .012 \*, r = .756, large); the RR group did not reach statistical significance under this matched sample (W = 3.0, p = .113, r = .647, large). Between-group gain score difference was not statistically significant.
(2) **Task completion rates**: Across T1–T5 tasks, none reached statistical significance between groups. T5 Fighter (autonomous hyperparameter setting) showed a descriptive gap of RR 61.1% vs Colab 25.0% with V = .286 (medium effect), but did not reach α = .05 under the current sample size.
(3) **Task completion time**: On T4 Heli (main assessment task), the RR group (17.6 min) was significantly faster than the Colab group (53.0 min), U = 0, p = .0013 \*\*, r = .813 (large). On T5 Fighter, RR (20.4 min) was also significantly faster than Colab (33.3 min), U = 0, p = .0115 \*, r = .687 (large), with the robustness caveat of Colab n = 3.
(4) **Subjective scales**: The 5-item Platform Feedback Scale, the System Usability Scale (SUS), and the NASA-TLX overall and most subscales did not reach statistical significance under the matched sample. The only significant subscale was NASA-TLX **Frustration**, where the RR group reported significantly higher frustration than the Colab group (U = 92.0, p = .034 \*, r = .450, medium).
(5) **Qualitative open-ended responses**: On "the most difficult part," Colab participants reported "parameter tuning difficulty" and "chart interpretation difficulty" at 42% each, 2.5× the rate of RR participants (17% each).

Based on these results, the contributions of this study are framed at three levels: (1) the construction of a browser-executable, zero-installation RL teaching platform Rein Room; (2) a standardized-intervention small-sample quasi-experimental comparison between two teaching interfaces, presenting RR's behavioral-level (completion time) efficiency advantage and concurrent subjective-level signals of information-density overload in the early version of RR; (3) five concrete design recommendations for future visual interactive teaching platforms (information density control, classroom mode, transfer ability assessment, real-time adjustment interface, and peer interaction mechanism). The statistical power of this study at the scale-level analyses is limited by sample size; conclusions should be interpreted within the scope of this study's context.

**Keywords:** Reinforcement Learning, Interactive Teaching Platform, Visual Learning, AI Education, Quasi-experimental Study, Rein Room
